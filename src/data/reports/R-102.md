# Smart Water Metering: Industry Outlook 2024

[[page 1]]
## Foreword
Aquila Insights presents this independent outlook on the smart water metering industry. The study focuses on the metering device market and the technology choices that determine its growth over the next five years.

[[page 2]]
## Scope of this Study
This outlook covers AMR and AMI metering hardware only. Software platforms, data analytics subscriptions and installation services are excluded from all market figures. Readers who need a full-stack view should treat the figures here as a lower bound on total spending.

[[page 3]]
## Methodology
Figures are built bottom-up from shipment volumes and average selling prices reported by manufacturers, cross-checked with utility tender awards. Primary interviews were held with meter manufacturers, distributors and utility procurement managers.

[[page 4]]
## Industry Structure
The metering hardware value chain runs from component suppliers through meter assemblers and communication module vendors to distributors and utilities. Tender-driven sales dominate, with long qualification cycles and multi-year framework agreements.

[[page 5]]
## Market Size
The global smart water meter hardware market, covering AMR and AMI devices, was valued at USD 3.87 billion in 2024. The market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 9.8% over the forecast window of 2024-2029.

[[page 6]]
## Forecast Assumptions
The forecast assumes stable raw material prices, continued public funding for water infrastructure and gradual migration from AMR to AMI. It does not model extreme scenarios such as supply chain shutdowns. The forecast window ends in 2029 and no values are provided beyond that year.

[[page 7]]
## AMR versus AMI
AMR devices remain attractive for budget-constrained utilities because they require simpler networks. AMI deployments carry higher unit prices but enable remote disconnection and pressure monitoring. Hybrid deployments are common during multi-year transitions.

[[page 8]]
## Communication Technologies
NB-IoT and LoRaWAN account for most new connected meters. Proprietary RF mesh persists in large legacy networks. Utilities increasingly prefer standards-based connectivity to avoid vendor lock-in.

[[page 9]]
## Regional Hardware Demand
Asia-Pacific held 41% of the 2024 hardware market, driven by high-volume tenders. Europe and North America follow with higher average selling prices but lower unit volumes. Latin America is an emerging market supported by loss-reduction programmes.

[[page 10]]
## Pricing Trends
Average selling prices for AMR meters continue to decline slowly while AMI prices stabilise as ultrasonic technology scales. Communication module costs have fallen due to competition among chipset suppliers.

[[page 11]]
## Supply Chain
Chip availability has normalised after recent shortages. Brass and polymer costs remain the main commodity exposures. Several manufacturers are localising assembly to meet domestic content rules in tenders.

[[page 12]]
## Competitive Dynamics
Competition centres on total cost of ownership, battery life and certification. Mid-sized regional manufacturers win on price in domestic tenders, while global players lead in large AMI programmes.

[[page 13]]
## Risks to the Outlook
Delays in public budgets, standards fragmentation and cybersecurity incidents are the principal downside risks. Upside comes from stricter leakage regulation and faster adoption of ultrasonic devices.

[[page 14]]
## Notes and Sources
Market figures were rounded to two decimal places. Sources include manufacturer filings, tender databases and expert interviews conducted between January and May 2024.
